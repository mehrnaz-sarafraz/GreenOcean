# Privacy and data rights

Group participation is sensitive contextual data, never a diagnosis. No marketing, external AI, new processor, sensitive recommendations or membership inference is introduced. Existing J purpose/consent governance remains authoritative. Private proposals, targeted invitations, rules acknowledgments, bans, transfers, event attendance and review evidence are server-owned and current-account scoped.

Account archive includes own proposals, sent/received invitation metadata, ownership offers, rules authored, sanctions against the account, attendance and group content actions/appeal outcomes. It excludes other participants' rosters and ordinary anonymous attribution. Removed members retain their own historical records without gaining other members' content.

Account erasure removes participation, attendance, targeted invites/transfers, own proposals, own group action/appeal narrative and sanctions against the account; actor references in retained group records are minimized. Erasing a current owner archives the owned group rather than assigning an arbitrary successor. Closure immediately stops ordinary interaction and revokes pending grants. It does not widen historical audiences or erase another member's narrative. No irreversible group mass-delete API is added.

New moderation records follow the existing J review/retention approval gate. No unapproved duration is invented for proposals, invitations after resolution, attendance, sanctions or appeals. Proposal intake/activation and new activity writes remain off for production until the actual processing/retention and reviewer arrangements are approved. Invitation validity is the selected configurable seven-day default; it is not a claim of physical deletion or a broader evidence retention policy.

Expiry reads are authoritative; resolved/expired invite IDs cannot revive membership. Draft retention approved for Phase L is separate (private seven days after last edit). Backup erasure replay, actual infrastructure retention, legal holds and disaster recovery need operational evidence; synthetic migration/export tests are not a compliance certification.
